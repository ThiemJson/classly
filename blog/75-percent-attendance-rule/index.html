What is the 75% attendance rule?

English · Tiếng Việt

Updated: 2026-10-15

A 75% attendance rule means you must attend at least three quarters of the classes counted by your institution; it is not one universal rule for every school.

What 75% means in numbers

Seventy-five percent means attending at least three of every four counted classes. Multiply the total classes by 0.75 and round up to find the minimum attendance. For 40 classes, 40 × 0.75 = 30, so you must attend at least 30 and can miss at most 10. For 45 classes, the minimum is 33.75, which rounds up to 34; you can miss 11.

The phrase “counted classes” matters. A policy may count each lecture, each hour, each laboratory, or each course meeting. It may apply per subject, across a semester, or only to a particular school within a university. Read the exact policy before relying on a calculation.

There is no single worldwide 75% rule

Official policies show why a universal answer would be misleading. Baylor University's College of Arts & Sciences says students must attend at least 75% of scheduled class meetings to earn course credit, while also allowing departments or instructors to set stricter requirements. Seton Hall Law School likewise states a 75% minimum for its day programme, but skills courses can have a stricter limit of two absences.

Guru Ghasidas University's BBA ordinance requires 75% of classes held during the semester, including lectures, practicals and tutorials, for end-semester examination eligibility. Its same ordinance describes a possible condonation route for attendance from 65% to below 75% when supported by specified reasons and documents. Those details belong to that programme and ordinance; they should not be copied to another institution.

How to check which rule applies to you

  1. Open your current academic regulations, student handbook and course syllabus.
  2. Search for “attendance,” “assiduité,” “frequency,” “shortage,” or “eligibility for examination.”
  3. Check whether the percentage applies per subject, per component such as laboratory work, or overall.
  4. Look for separate treatment of justified absences, official activities and medical leave.
  5. Ask the department or registrar if the written rule is unclear, and keep their answer.

Does 74.5% round up?

Do not assume it does. Mathematically, 74.5% is below 75%. Some systems may display a rounded whole number while eligibility is evaluated using the unrounded value. Unless your institution explicitly says that it rounds for eligibility, plan using the exact percentage.

How to stay safely above the line

Calculate each subject separately and leave a margin for illness or timetable changes. If your target is 75%, being exactly at 75% means the next absence puts you below it. At 34 attended out of 45, for example, you have 75.55%; one more missed class produces 34 out of 46, or 73.91%.

Use the table for planning, but treat your institution's document as the authority. A calculator can apply the percentage correctly; it cannot decide which classes, exceptions or penalties your school counts.

Does medical leave automatically protect your attendance?

Not necessarily. A medical certificate can document why you were absent, but the effect depends on the policy that applies to you. Some institutions provide a formal condonation or exception process within a limited shortage range; others include excused absences in the numerical total while handling their consequences separately. Submit documents within the stated deadline and ask whether the absence is removed, excused, or merely considered in a later decision.

Per-subject and overall attendance are different

Suppose you attended 28 of 30 classes in one subject and 20 of 30 in another. Overall, you attended 48 of 60, or 80%. The second subject, however, is only 66.66%. If the rule applies independently to each course, the healthy overall number does not solve the shortage. This is why the safest tracker keeps separate held and attended counts for every subject, laboratory and tutorial that has its own requirement.

A responsible way to use the allowed absence margin

The 25% gap is a maximum under a 75% rule, not a recommendation to use every absence. Timetables change, teachers may add make-up meetings, and illness can arrive late in the semester. Keep a buffer, confirm that attendance records are correct, and avoid planning an absence when the next missed class would move you below the line.

Common questions

Is the 75% attendance rule a law everywhere?

No. It is a policy used by some institutions and programmes. Your own syllabus, handbook or university regulation controls your case.

How many classes can I miss with a 75% rule?

You can miss at most 25% of the counted classes. For 40 classes, that is 10, provided you attend the other 30.
