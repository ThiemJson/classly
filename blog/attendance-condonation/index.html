What is attendance condonation?

English · Tiếng Việt

Updated: 2026-10-15

Attendance condonation is a formal, institution-specific decision to excuse a limited shortage for eligibility purposes; it is not an automatic right or a universal rule.

Condonation is a policy decision, not a calculation

In attendance rules, condonation generally means that an authorized person or body may accept a shortage within defined limits. It can affect eligibility to sit an examination or continue a programme, but the exact effect comes from the institution's ordinance. A calculator can measure the shortage; it cannot decide whether it will be condoned.

The term is especially common in some Indian university regulations, but even there the rules differ by university, programme and regulation year. Never copy a percentage or fee from another college.

Examples of different official rules

Guru Ghasidas University's BBA ordinance states a 75% requirement and says students with at least 65% but below 75% may apply through the Head of Department to the Dean. It lists reasons such as specified university activities, educational excursions, prolonged illness and other reasons satisfactory to the responsible authorities, supported by documents.

Mangalore University's published ordinances show why programme context matters: one section describes condonation for attendance from 60% to below 75% on payment of a prescribed fee, while the postgraduate section shown in the same document requires 75% in each paper and says there is no provision for condonation. Osmania University College's attendance page describes condonation up to a specified deficiency in special cases on medical grounds, with recommendation, documentation and a fee. These are examples, not a combined rule.

What an application may require

  • A percentage within the permitted shortage band.
  • A stated reason recognized by the applicable regulation.
  • Medical certificates or evidence of approved activities.
  • Recommendation from an instructor, head, principal or dean.
  • Submission before a fixed deadline.
  • A prescribed fee, where the institution's rules provide one.

Meeting these conditions may only make you eligible to apply. Words such as “may condone” usually leave a decision to the named authority.

How to find your real rule

  1. Identify your university, affiliated college, programme and regulation year.
  2. Find the official ordinance or academic regulations on the institution's own domain.
  3. Check whether attendance is calculated overall, per subject, or separately for theory and practical work.
  4. Read the minimum percentage, condonable range, accepted reasons and deadline together.
  5. Confirm the current form and process with the examination or academic office.

A social-media post or a senior student's experience may refer to an older regulation. Keep a copy of the document and the version that applies to your admission cohort.

What to do if you are below the limit

First verify the attendance record for errors. Then calculate the best possible final percentage using the classes remaining. If ordinary attendance can restore the target, create a recovery plan. If not, contact the responsible office immediately rather than waiting for hall-ticket or examination decisions.

Prepare documents honestly and do not assume a medical certificate automatically erases absences. Ask whether condonation changes only eligibility or also the recorded percentage, and get the decision in writing when possible.

Condonation versus make-up classes

A make-up class can change the numerical record if it is officially counted as a class you attended. Condonation may instead accept a shortage without adding a presence. Because the mechanisms are different, record only official make-up attendance in your calculator and track the administrative application separately.

Example of measuring a shortage

If you attended 26 of 40 classes, your attendance is 65%. A 75% requirement at that point would mean at least 30 attended, so the current shortage is four classes. That calculation does not show whether you qualify for condonation. The relevant ordinance might use a percentage band, require remaining classes to be completed, exclude a programme, or give the authority full discretion.

If 20 classes remain, ordinary recovery may still be possible. To finish the 60-class total at 75%, you need 45 attended overall. Starting from 26, that means attending 19 of the remaining 20. Showing both the current shortage and the best recovery plan makes a conversation with the department more concrete.

Avoid common assumptions

  • Do not assume paying a fee guarantees approval.
  • Do not assume every medical absence falls inside the permitted band.
  • Do not use another programme's rule merely because it belongs to the same university.
  • Do not wait for the examination schedule to collect documents.

Common questions

Is attendance condonation guaranteed?

No. Where it exists, it normally requires eligibility, an application, evidence and approval by the authority named in the institution's rules.

Does condonation increase my actual attendance percentage?

Not necessarily. It may permit an exception for eligibility without changing the historical record of classes attended.
