Does medical leave count in attendance?

Updated: 2026-10-15

It depends on your institution: a medical certificate may excuse an absence, support an exception request, or leave the numerical absence unchanged.

“Excused” does not always mean “not counted”

Attendance policies often separate two questions: whether an absence has a valid reason, and whether it remains in the numerical attendance total. A medical certificate can answer the first question without automatically changing the second. Your portal might still show an absence while the institution considers it differently for penalties, make-up work or examination eligibility.

Official policies differ. The University of Richmond says most non-university absences, including illness, may be excused at faculty discretion and notes that its Student Health Center does not provide medical excuse notes. Seton Hall Law School's day-program policy permits up to 25% absence without requiring students to provide a reason, while still requiring at least 75% attendance for credit. Guru Ghasidas University's BBA ordinance lists prolonged illness supported by a registered medical practitioner among reasons that may support condonation for attendance from 65% to below 75%. These examples describe their own programmes, not a general rule.

Four possible outcomes

  1. The absence stays counted: the certificate documents the reason but does not raise your percentage.
  2. The absence is excused: it may still appear in the record but avoid a separate penalty.
  3. You receive make-up work or attendance: only an officially recorded replacement changes the relevant count.
  4. You may apply for an exception: a formal authority reviews evidence and decides whether a shortage can be condoned.

Do not assume which outcome applies from the word “medical.” Look for the exact verbs in the policy: excluded, excused, waived, condoned, or eligible to apply.

What to do when you become ill

  1. Notify the instructor or required office as early as possible.
  2. Ask what evidence is accepted and the deadline for submitting it.
  3. Keep the original certificate and a copy of every submission.
  4. Ask whether the absence remains in the held/attended calculation.
  5. Check the portal after the decision and report discrepancies promptly.

A late, otherwise valid document can fail if the rules impose a submission window. If you cannot attend in person, ask whether an electronic notice is required before you return.

How to calculate while waiting for a decision

Use the conservative version first: count every class that took place as held and count only classes you actually attended as attended. For example, if 30 classes were held and you attended 22, the result is 73.33%. If the institution later removes one medically excused meeting from the denominator, the result becomes 22 of 29, or 75.86%. Do not use the second number until the record is officially changed.

Questions to ask the right office

  • Does a justified medical absence remain in the attendance percentage?
  • Is approval automatic or discretionary?
  • Who makes the decision: instructor, department, dean or registrar?
  • Are laboratories, placements or practicals treated differently?
  • Can missed work or a class be made up, and will that alter attendance?

The safest answer is the current written rule plus confirmation from the office responsible for applying it. A calculator can show both numerical scenarios, but it cannot grant an exception.

Keep attendance and medical records separate

Maintain one record of what physically happened—class held, present, absent or cancelled—and another record of documents submitted and decisions received. Changing your personal attendance count before approval can make your forecast too optimistic. When an office confirms a correction, note the date, the person or system that confirmed it and the revised total.

This separation also helps when different consequences use different definitions. An instructor may allow missed coursework to be completed while the registrar still counts the absence, or a department may approve examination eligibility without altering the portal percentage. Both outcomes can be valid under the same process.

If the policy is unclear

Send a short written question containing the course, date of absence, current record and type of document you have. Ask for the applicable clause rather than relying only on an informal assurance. Written clarification reduces misunderstandings and gives the office enough detail to direct you to the correct process.

Common questions

Does a medical certificate erase an absence?

Not automatically. It proves a reason, but the applicable policy decides whether the absence is removed, excused or considered in an exception process.

Who should I ask about medical attendance?

Ask the course instructor, department or registrar and request the current written attendance rule and submission deadline.
