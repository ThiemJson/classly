How to increase attendance from 60% to 75%

Updated: 2026-10-15

To move from 60% to 75%, you must attend new classes without missing any; for example, 24 attended out of 40 requires 24 consecutive classes to reach 48 out of 64.

The recovery formula

classes needed = ⌈ (target × held − attended) ÷ (1 − target) ⌉

Write 75% as 0.75. If 40 classes were held and you attended 24, your attendance is 60%. The calculation is (0.75 × 40 − 24) ÷ 0.25 = 24. After attending 24 consecutive classes, you reach 48 attended out of 64 held, exactly 75%.

This answer surprises many students because attending a class changes both numbers. Starting at 24 of 40, ten new presences would produce 34 of 50, which is only 68%. You cannot add 15 percentage points directly.

At an exact 60% starting rate, the number of consecutive classes required equals 60% of the classes already held. The general formula is safer because real records are not always exactly 60%.

Check whether enough classes remain

First find the best possible final rate: add every remaining class to both attended and held. If you have 24 of 40 and only 15 classes remain, perfect attendance gives 39 of 55, or 70.90%. Reaching 75% is mathematically impossible within that schedule. If 30 classes remain, you need 29 of them because 0.75 × 70 − 24 = 28.5, rounded up to 29.

A practical recovery plan

  1. Verify the held and attended counts with the official portal or instructor.
  2. Check the target and whether it applies separately to each subject.
  3. Count realistic remaining meetings, excluding known holidays and cancellations.
  4. Calculate both the consecutive classes needed and the end-of-semester minimum.
  5. Update the calculation after every absence, cancellation or extra class.

If the target is no longer reachable, contact the appropriate department early. Medical leave, make-up work or attendance condonation—where these exist—are policy decisions, not mathematical adjustments a calculator can promise.

Avoid another drop

Once you reach exactly 75%, you have no buffer: missing the next class puts you below target. Continue until you have enough margin for at least one absence. Track every subject independently, set reminders before class and resolve discrepancies while attendance records are still recent.

Example with a missed class during recovery

Start again at 24 attended out of 40 held. The uninterrupted route requires 24 presences. If you attend 10 classes and then miss one, your record becomes 34 of 51, or 66.66%. Recalculate from that point: (0.75 × 51 − 34) ÷ 0.25 = 17. The single new absence adds more than one class to the original journey because it lowers the rate and increases the total you must overcome.

Can extra or make-up classes help?

They help only when the institution records them as classes you attended. An informal study session does not change official attendance. Before relying on a make-up class, ask whether it will appear in the same subject's held and attended totals. If it replaces a cancelled session, the final effect may differ from an additional class.

What to ask your department

Bring the exact record rather than only saying “I have 60%.” Ask how many classes remain, whether laboratories are counted separately, how justified absences are treated, and when eligibility is evaluated. If an exception process exists, ask for the written rule, documents and deadline. A clear answer lets you separate the policy question from the arithmetic and make a realistic plan.

Most importantly, do not wait for the final week. At low attendance, the remaining timetable is a hard mathematical limit. Checking early gives you more ordinary classes to attend and more time to correct any recording errors.

Common questions

Why does 60% to 75% take so many classes?

Each new class increases both the attended count and the total held, so the percentage rises gradually.

Can one missed class during recovery change the answer?

Yes. A missed class increases only the total held, so recalculate whenever you miss or a class is added.
