Subject-wise vs overall attendance

Updated: 2026-10-15

Subject-wise attendance is calculated separately for each subject, while overall attendance combines all subjects; a good overall rate can still hide one subject below its target.

The two formulas

Subject-wise attendance uses only the classes for one subject:

subject attendance = subject classes attended ÷ subject classes held × 100

Overall attendance adds the attended and held counts from every subject before dividing:

overall attendance = total attended across subjects ÷ total held across subjects × 100

Do not average the displayed percentages unless every subject has exactly the same number of classes. A subject with 40 meetings must carry more weight than one with 10 meetings in the true overall calculation.

A worked example

SubjectAttendedHeldAttendance
Mathematics263086.66%
Physics213070%
Chemistry222491.66%

The combined record is 69 attended out of 84 held, or 82.14%. That overall number is comfortably above a 75% target, yet Physics is only 70%. If the institution applies its requirement per subject, the student still has a Physics shortage.

To recover Physics from 21 of 30 to 75%, the student must attend 6 consecutive Physics classes, reaching 27 of 36. Attending Mathematics or Chemistry does not change the Physics percentage.

Why overall attendance can be misleading

Subjects do not always meet equally often. A daily lecture with strong attendance can dominate the combined total, while a weekly laboratory with two missed sessions falls below its own limit. Overall attendance is useful as a broad habit indicator, but it should not replace the unit used for academic eligibility.

The opposite can also happen. Your overall percentage may look weak because one large subject has many absences, even though several smaller subjects are safe. A subject table shows exactly where action is needed.

How to track subject-wise attendance

  1. Create one row for every subject and any separately assessed laboratory or tutorial.
  2. Store classes held and attended, not only a rounded percentage.
  3. Use the correct target for that subject.
  4. Update the row after every meeting, cancellation or correction.
  5. Compare it with the official portal each week.

If a two-hour block is recorded as two periods, use two in both held and attended. If it is one meeting, use one. Consistent units matter more than the label.

Should laboratories be separate?

Track them separately whenever the syllabus, portal or regulations show a separate requirement. Even if you also calculate a combined course percentage, retaining separate counts lets you respond if practical attendance is checked independently.

A safer dashboard

Show the overall rate, then sort subjects by risk: below target, at the edge, and safe. For each subject, calculate either classes you can miss or consecutive classes needed. This turns a single reassuring number into an actionable plan.

Until you confirm the institution's method, assume subject-wise requirements matter and preserve both views. The subject-wise attendance calculator makes the comparison in one table.

How weighted overall attendance works

Suppose Subject A has 9 attended out of 10 and Subject B has 27 out of 40. A simple average of 90% and 67.5% is 78.75%, but that is not the true combined rate. Add the counts: 36 attended out of 50 held equals 72%. The larger subject contributes four times as many meetings and must therefore have four times the weight.

This distinction matters whenever course schedules differ. Always sum raw counts for the overall figure and retain the individual rows for eligibility checks.

How cancellations affect the views

A cancelled class normally changes neither attended nor held. If an extra class replaces it, record the extra occurrence when it happens. Updating the correct subject preserves both that subject's percentage and the combined total. Moving a cancellation into the wrong row can make one subject appear safe while another looks worse.

What to do when the portal shows only overall attendance

Keep your own subject-level record and ask whether detailed counts are available from instructors or the department. Do not infer that an overall dashboard means the official rule is also overall. The written policy and assessment structure determine the relevant unit.

Common questions

Can overall attendance be high while one subject is low?

Yes. High attendance in frequently scheduled subjects can outweigh a shortage in another subject when all classes are combined.

Which attendance number should I follow?

Follow the unit used by your institution's written rule. When uncertain, track both overall and each subject separately.
