How bunking changes your attendance

A bunked class still counts as held, so your percentage falls every time. The limit for a target is:

bunks left = ⌊ attended × 100 ÷ target − held ⌋

With 34 of 40 classes attended (85%) and a 75% rule: 34 × 100 ÷ 75 − 40 = 5.33, so you can bunk 5 classes. After those you would be at 34 ÷ 45 = 75.55%. A sixth bunk would take you to 34 ÷ 46 = 73.91%.

The one-in-four rule of thumb

For a 75% minimum, at least three of every four classes must be attended. So the most you can ever have missed is a third of the classes you attended: 30 attended allows 10 missed, 45 attended allows 15. The table on the 75% attendance calculator lists it for common class counts.

Before you bunk

  • Calculate per subject. A safe overall percentage can hide one subject below the line.
  • Keep one or two classes in reserve for days you are ill. Medical leave is not always approved.
  • Check whether your college rounds 74.5% up. Many do not.

Common questions

How many classes can I bunk with 75% attendance?

At most one class for every three you attend. Exactly: bunks allowed = attended × 100 ÷ 75 − held, rounded down. With 34 of 40 attended, that is 5 more classes.

Is it better to bunk early or late in the semester?

Late, if at all. Early in the term each class is a big share of the total, so a single absence moves your percentage a lot, and you do not yet know how many classes will be cancelled or how strict the subject is.

Do labs and tutorials count separately?

At many colleges, yes: a lab or tutorial is often its own subject with its own attendance. Check how your timetable lists it and calculate each one separately.

Does a bunk on a day with two classes count twice?

If the subject meets twice that day, both classes are held and both are missed, so it counts as two. Enter classes, not days.
