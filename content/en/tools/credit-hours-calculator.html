Worked example: 3 US semester credits

Under the US federal benchmark, one semester credit reasonably approximates at least one hour of instruction plus two hours of out-of-class work each week for about 15 weeks. A 3-credit course therefore represents about 9 total hours per week: roughly 3 hours in class and at least 6 hours outside class.

The rule also permits equivalent work over a different period and lets institutions account for delivery method, discipline and degree level. Use the calculator as a planning estimate, then check the workload stated in your syllabus.

Worked example: 6 ECTS

The ECTS Users' Guide says one credit usually corresponds to 25–30 hours of total work. A 6-ECTS course therefore represents 150–180 hours. Spread across 15 teaching weeks, that is about 10–12 hours per week, including lectures, seminars, projects, practical work and individual study.

What the calculator includes

  • US semester credits: the calculator derives about 45 total hours per credit from the 15-week federal benchmark and shows the estimated out-of-class portion separately.
  • ECTS: it shows a range because the official guide gives 25–30 workload hours per credit, not one universal number.
  • Custom: enter the total hours per credit published by your institution, then divide that workload across your actual teaching weeks.

These are workload estimates, not promises about how long every student will need. Labs, placements, compressed terms and institution-specific rules can produce a different weekly pattern.
